# Tutorial Video Scripts

Three short scripts (60–90 seconds each) to record with OBS. Each has a shot list on the left of the dash and the voice-over in quotes. Timings are targets; the auto editor's silence cuts will tighten them further.

Recording tips:
- Record the screen at 1920 × 1080, browser in full screen (F11), zoom at 100%.
- Use a starter world or a fresh one with a clear name; close other tabs.
- Read from the hub's **Teleprompter** tab; tap to start/stop recording.
- Keep claims to what the viewer sees on screen. No ratings, review quotes or sales numbers.

---

## Video 1: "Your first game in 60 seconds" (about 60 s)

**Goal:** show a non-technical viewer that they can make something playable right away.

| Time | On screen | Voice-over |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:05 | Drone flyover of Firefly Fen (press P) | "This is Stark World Engine: a game builder that runs in your browser, on your own computer." |
| 0:05–0:12 | Double-click `START_STARK_WORLD_ENGINE.bat`, setup check passes, browser opens | "Unzip it, double-click Start, and the studio opens. No account, no internet, and on Windows nothing else to install." |
| 0:12–0:20 | Click **New 3D world**, type "Frost Island" | "Let's start fresh. New 3D world, give it a name." |
| 0:20–0:28 | Click **Sub-Zero Aurora**, then **Crimson Citadel**, settle on Aurora | "One click changes the whole mood: sky, fog, light and weather." |
| 0:28–0:40 | Tools: place trees, rocks, 5 crystals, 1 Slime | "Pick a tool and click to place. Crystals are what your player collects. Creatures keep it interesting." |
| 0:40–0:52 | **Save**, **▶ Play**: walk, jump, bonk the slime, collect | "Save, hit Play, and it's a game: walk, jump, fight, collect." |
| 0:52–1:00 | "World complete!" then **Export > HTML5 cartridge**, file list | "Finish it, export one HTML file, and send your game to anyone. That's it: your first game." |

## Video 2: "Pixel hero + custom sounds for a 2D platformer" (about 75 s)

**Goal:** show the 2D builder, the Pixel Art canvas and the SFX Forge together.

| Time | On screen | Voice-over |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:06 | Lantern Leap gameplay | "Stark World Engine does 2D too. Let's build a platformer with our own hero and our own sounds." |
| 0:06–0:15 | **New 2D world**, name "Pearl Cove" | "New 2D world. You start with ground, a start point and a goal." |
| 0:15–0:28 | Paint ground platforms, pearls, a spike, a slime | "Paint tiles: ground to stand on, pearls to collect, spikes and slimes for danger." |
| 0:28–0:42 | **Pixel Art** tab: draw a 16 × 16 hero, **Use as player sprite** | "Now the hero. Sixteen by sixteen pixels, a few colors, and it's ours." |
| 0:42–0:58 | **SFX Forge**: preset *coin*, wave *sine*, move freq, **Play**, **Save WAV** | "Sounds next. Start from a preset, shape it with sliders, listen, and save. It becomes the in-game sound." |
| 0:58–1:10 | **Save**, **▶ Play**: run, jump, stomp, collect (sound audible) | "Save and play. Hear that? That's the sound we just made." |
| 1:10–1:15 | Reach the flag | "A whole platformer, hero and sounds included, in about five minutes." |

## Video 3: "From browser to Godot 4" (about 85 s, Pro / Studio)

**Goal:** honestly show what the Godot export gives you and how to open it.

| Time | On screen | Voice-over |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:08 | Firefly Fen in the studio, drone mode | "When you're ready for a full engine, Stark World Engine Pro and Studio can export your world to Godot 4." |
| 0:08–0:16 | **Export** tab, paste key, **Activate** | "First, activate: paste your key. It's checked right on your computer. Nothing is sent anywhere." |
| 0:16–0:26 | Click **Godot 4 project**, show the file list | "Click Godot 4 project. You get a project file, a scene, terrain and player scripts, and your world data." |
| 0:26–0:42 | Godot Project Manager: **Import** > `project.godot` > **Import & Edit** | "In Godot 4.2 or newer: Import, pick project dot godot, Import and Edit." |
| 0:42–0:55 | Godot editor showing the lit scene, press F5, walk around | "Press F5, and you're walking through your first level in Godot: same layout, with sky, fog, glow and your mood's light." |
| 0:55–1:10 | Select the `Props` node, drag a tree model into its tree slot | "Be clear on what this is: a starting scene. Objects arrive as simple shapes, and the quests and combat stay in the browser version. Swap in your own models, add your own gameplay." |
| 1:10–1:25 | Side by side: browser version vs Godot version | "So you go from idea to playable in the browser, then carry the layout into a full engine without rebuilding it by hand." |
