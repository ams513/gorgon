# Proposal: child-safety clause for the Stark World Engine licence / terms of sale

This is for the lead and owner to review. It is not legal advice, so have it checked before it goes into the shipped
licence. The short version is already in INTEGRATION_PATCHES.md as patch P9b (`personal_license()` item 6).

## Licence clause (PERSONAL_LICENSE.txt, store terms)

> **Prohibited content.** You may not use Stark World Engine, or anything it produces, to create, export, distribute or
> promote content that sexualises minors or depicts, describes or encourages the abuse of minors. "Minor" means anyone
> under 18, including fictional, drawn or animated characters who are, appear to be, or are described as under 18,
> whatever age the story gives them. Any use of this kind **terminates this licence immediately and automatically**,
> without refund, and you must stop using and delete every copy of Stark World Engine. Stark World Engine refuses to export
> such content and exported games refuse to run it; trying to get around those protections (by editing exported files,
> renaming characters, misspelling words or any other means) is itself a breach of this clause. Where the law requires
> it, we report child sexual abuse material to the authorities.

## Store page line (short)

> Stark World Engine has built-in child-safety rules that cannot be turned off: child characters cannot be harmed or
> romanced, and the studio refuses to export sexual or abusive content involving minors. Using it to create such
> content ends your licence.

## Notes for the owner

* The clause names fictional and "appears to be" minors on purpose, so the "1000-year-old who looks 12" excuse fails.
  The checker enforces the same idea (inference, CS012).
* "Where the law requires it, we report…": keep it only if you are prepared to act on it. Reporting duties differ by
  country, so check with counsel.
* A refund exclusion may conflict with consumer law in some regions (the EU, for example). Counsel should adjust the
  wording per storefront. itch.io is the merchant of record and has its own content rules, which this clause sits
  alongside.
