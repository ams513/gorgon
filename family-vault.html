    About Family Vault

    Family Vault is a private, offline place for the papers and facts your family would need in a bad week. Everything is encrypted on your computer. There is no account, no server, no analytics and no network access at all.

    New in version 2.0.0: a number for what you kept, a weekly look, a spreadsheet, details that stay right

    • Kept or recovered this year. When there is something to show, Home opens with one number: claims you marked as paid this year, the yearly price of subscriptions you cancelled, and the late fee that was at stake on papers you renewed before they expired. "How this is counted" explains it. Only amounts you typed are used; the late-fee part is an upper limit; nothing is read from a bank or a website.
    • Still right? Emergency contacts, medicine list, blood type and allergies, and insurance details are the facts that hurt most when they are old. Home asks, at most two at a time, once a detail has not been confirmed for 6 months (medicines) or a year (the rest). "Yes, still right" notes the date; "Open it" goes to the page. It only asks about pages that hold something.
    • Weekly check-in. One button on Home, "I looked at this week", and a count of weeks in a row. It is a reminder habit, not a score: it changes nothing else.
    • Spreadsheet (.csv). Under Sharing and Reminders: one file with document expiry dates and renewal fees, insurance renewals and premiums, medicine run-out dates, claims, subscriptions and your repeating checks. It never includes document numbers, account details, health notes or secure notes. The file is not encrypted. (The calendar file, .ics, and the printouts are unchanged.)
    • Phone Medical ID text. On a person's Emergency card, "Copy as text" puts the same facts as plain text on the clipboard, to paste into your phone's emergency note. Copying needs a browser that allows it; otherwise you are told.
    • Old backup files open. A test opens backup files written by versions 1.6 and 1.8 in this version and checks every record. The vault format is unchanged.
    • Look and feel. Home uses the shared Stark kit for the number and the rows; older pages keep their layout with the same colours.
    • Screen languages: the new short labels are English for now; the 15 translations are unchanged. The new settings (confirmed dates, weekly check-ins) are saved in your backups, mirror and Beam; like the claims, they are not carried by merge-in, share files or packs.

    New in version 1.9.0: a guided first run, a trip check, checklists for hard days, a yearly review

    • Guided first run. A new vault opens with a five-step card on Home (family, a document with an expiry date, a policy, two emergency contacts, a backup). One button, "Try it with an example family", fills the vault with made-up records marked (example) so every page shows how it works; "Remove the example" takes out exactly those records and nothing you typed.
    • Trip check (Alt+T). Choose the first and last day and who is going. It reads only what is in the vault: each passport against the trip end (and whether 6 months remain), each medicine's counted supply against the days away, whether a travel policy is listed and does not end early, fewer than two emergency contacts, and every date that falls before or during the trip. Worst problems first, print it, and Home shows how many things are still open as the trip gets close. It is a checklist, not travel advice: entry rules differ by country.
    • When it happens (Alt+L). Five tickable checklists with plain steps and a link to the page that helps: lost wallet or phone, moving house, a new baby, losing or changing a job, and after a death in the family. Ticks are saved in the encrypted vault, each checklist prints, and Home shows the one you started. General steps only, not legal, tax or medical advice.
    • Yearly review (Alt+Y). One page and one printout: what is overdue, everything dated in the next 12 months, what insurance premiums and subscriptions cost a year, claims filed and recovered, renewal fees coming, and what is still missing from your readiness list.
    • Look and feel. Stark deep blue and silver in light and dark, five main pages plus a More menu (a bottom bar on phones), 44 px tap targets, visible keyboard focus, a skip link, and print layouts that do not split rows across pages. Thousands separators on money, as before.
    • Screen languages. The new short labels and buttons are translated in the 15 languages (machine drafts, not reviewed by native speakers); the checklist steps and longer sentences stay English for now.
    • New shortcuts: Alt+T, Alt+L, Alt+Y. Claims, subscriptions, trip dates and checklist ticks are saved in your backups, mirror and Beam; as before they are not carried by merge-in, share files or packs. Saved data stays in the same encrypted format.

    New in version 1.8.0: money kept, medicines that do not run out, a bad-day backup test

    • Claims. A Claims page for every insurance claim: what happened, the policy, the amount, a follow-up date, and Mark paid or Denied. Home shows how much is still owed and how much came back this year, and the follow-up date appears in This week. It records; it does not contact anyone.
    • Subscriptions and free trials. A Subscriptions page with the price, how often it charges and the next date. It totals the yearly cost, shows what charges in the next 7 days, reminds you 3 days before a free trial's cancel-by date, and counts what you save when you mark one cancelled. The vault cannot see your bank: it only knows what you type.
    • Medicine supply. On a medicine, "Track pills left" takes the pills on hand and the number taken per day and works out the day they run out. That date joins Reminders and This week, so the refill is a week early instead of a missed dose. "Picked up a refill" adds to the count.
    • Sitter sheet (Alt+S). On Health, one page for whoever looks after the children or the house: emergency contacts, address, each child's allergies, conditions, medicines and doctor, pets, your house rules and any notes you choose. Print it for the fridge. Printed copies are not encrypted.
    • Test a backup file. On Sharing, choose a backup and its passphrase: the app opens it, counts what is inside and says whether everything in your vault today is in it. Nothing is changed. Home reminds you when no backup has been tested, or the last test was over 180 days ago.
    • New shortcuts: Alt+C new claim, Alt+U new subscription, Alt+S sitter sheet. English only for now, like the 1.7.0 screens; the saved data stays in the same encrypted vault and old backups open unchanged.

    New in version 1.7.0: a reason to open it every week

    • This week on Home. The first screen now shows what is overdue or due in the next 7 days, a button for the first thing to do, and a "Print for the fridge" checklist. If you give a document a late fee (Edit, "Late fee or penalty if missed"), Home adds up the late fees a missed date could cost in the next 30 days. When you press Mark renewed the vault records whether it was on time, and shows this year's count and the late fees it could have cost.
    • Emergency cards as a PDF. On Health, "Wallet cards (PDF)" saves one file with a wallet-size card for everyone in the vault (blood type, allergies, medicines, conditions, doctor, insurer, and up to three emergency contacts), with cut lines. The PDF writer is built in; it needs no network. Text outside Latin-1 (for example Chinese names) prints as "?" in the PDF; use the existing Print card button for those. The PDF file is not encrypted.
    • Secure notes. A Notes page for plain text kept inside the encrypted vault: where the shut-offs are, babysitter rules, vet details. Pin, search, print one note.     New in version 1.6.0: Beam, a vault to another device with no network

    • Beam to another device. On the Sharing page, one device shows a moving sequence of codes and the other reads them with its camera. No network, no cable, no file to move: it works in a basement with no signal. Only your vault backup is shown, already encrypted with your vault passphrase, in pieces that look like noise. You need your passphrase on the other device, and a six-digit pairing code on both screens shows you are reading the right one.
    • Where it lands. On the other device's lock screen choose Other ways in, then Receive from another device: it opens the same restore step as a backup file (it replaces the vault on that computer, as a restore always does). In a vault that is already open, Receive and merge in (full version) shows what is new and what differs before anything is changed.
    • Honest limits. Beam moves a copy and does not keep two devices in sync. Camera quality and light matter: hold the camera steady 20 to 30 cm away. A vault without scans or photos is about 6 to 35 KB, so 4 to 24 seconds of codes; each MB of scans and photos adds about 12 minutes at the slower speed, so for a vault full of scans a backup file on a USB drive is the better way. Browsers without their own code reader use a slower built-in one. Tested here with a recording played as a fake camera, not with a real phone in a real room.
    • Free trial. Receiving is in the free trial; sending (and merge-in) is in the full version.

    New in version 1.5.0: hand-over and merge

    • Emergency access pack for a named person. On the Sharing page, prepare a sealed copy for one trusted person. You choose the people and sections they may see; the app seals them into an encrypted file with a passphrase it makes up (20 letters and digits, about 100 bits, never your vault passphrase) and writes an instruction sheet in plain words: what the pack is, what they will be able to see, what they need and how to open it, step by step. Print it, or save the sheet as a text file to sit beside the pack file.
    • One card, or pack codes held by several people. The pack opens either with one printed passphrase card, or with pack codes: the passphrase is split with the same scheme as the Recovery kit into 2 to 5 codes, each on its own printed card, and the file opens only when the number you chose (say 2 of 3: the person, your attorney, an envelope at home) are brought together. One code alone reveals nothing.
    • Opened from the lock screen. The person chooses Other ways in, then Open an emergency access pack, picks the file and types the passphrase or the codes. The records open to read; nothing is saved on that computer. A check on the same page lets you try a pack before you hand it over.
    • What a pack is not. Nothing releases it by itself: there is no timer, no check-in and no server. Whoever holds the file and the passphrase, or enough codes, can open it at any time. It cannot be cancelled from a distance, it is a copy from the day it was made, and it is not a will, a power of attorney or any legal document. The printed sheet and cards are not encrypted. The app keeps only who, when and how, never the passphrase or the codes.
    • Merge in a share file or a backup, record by record. Two copies of a vault that were edited apart can be brought together: choose the other copy's share file or backup, and see what is new, what differs (field by field) and which copy changed last before anything is changed. For each record choose keep mine, take the file's copy, or keep both; when the app cannot tell which is newer it keeps both. Then you get a report of what was done, an Undo while the report is open, and the report as a text file if you want it.
    • Edit times. From 1.5.0 the vault notes when each record last changed (inside the encrypted vault, by this computer's clock) so a merge can suggest the newer copy. Records last changed in an earlier version have no edit time and are treated as "cannot tell".
    • It is a one-time merge of a file, not sync. The other copy is never changed, and deleting a record on one copy does not delete it on the other: it shows up as new. A file that left scans or password hints out never erases yours. This replaces the older "import a share file" button, which added everything as new entries.

    New in version 1.3.0: harder to lose, harder to break

    • Live mirror file. Choose a file on a USB stick or in a folder your own backup software looks after, and after every save the vault writes the same encrypted file there. Clearing the browser no longer costs you the vault: restore the mirror from the lock screen with your passphrase. Only the encrypted file is ever written. Works in current Chrome and Edge on a computer when you run the downloaded app (browsers do not allow it inside this store page); other browsers say so and you keep using backup files.
    • Storage protection. See how much of the browser's storage the vault uses, and ask the browser not to clear it under low disk space (the browser may still say no, and clearing site data by hand still erases it).
    • How long would a guess take? A live simulator: type a passphrase in the same style as yours (it is never saved) and see rough average guessing times for four kinds of attacker, from one stolen laptop to a very well funded lab, with the vault's slow key and, for contrast, with a single fast hash. The passphrase box shows the same estimate as you type. These are honest orders of magnitude, not promises.
    • Stronger by default. New vaults, new passphrases and share files now need a passphrase rated Strong (12 or more characters). A vault restored from an older, lower-cost file is re-encrypted at full strength on unlock, and files asking for an unreasonable key cost are refused.
    • Rollback warning. Each save carries a counter sealed into the encrypted data, and the browser remembers the highest one it has seen. If an older copy of the vault is put back in this browser's storage in place of a newer one, the vault tells you when you unlock. It cannot notice if the whole browser profile was put back, so keep backup files.
    • Saves you can trust. Saves run one at a time, a failed save always says so (and the vault will not auto-lock over unsaved changes), and error messages stay on screen until you dismiss them.
    • Easier to read and use. Higher-contrast colours in the light theme, nothing smaller than 12 px, form errors linked to the field that needs fixing, and screen readers hear the answer to a question instead of the whole list.

    New in version 1.2.0: the vault that thinks

    All of this runs on your computer, from your own records. Still no account, no server, no network.

    • Ask your vault. Type a question in your own words: "what expires in the next 90 days?", "how much do we pay for insurance?", "what is in the kitchen worth?", "which medicines does Sam take?", "phone number for Jordan". You get a one-line answer and the records behind it. It reads what you have recorded and nothing else.
    • Quick find (Ctrl+K). A keyboard-first command palette: jump to any section, lock the vault, or search every record with typo-tolerant fuzzy matching ("pasport", "sumit life"). Ask a question there and it hands you the answer. Password hints are never searched.
    • The year ahead. A 12-month chart of every document expiry, renewal, refill, warranty and repeating check, with your premiums drawn as a line. It flags pile-up months (too many dates at once) and heavy premium months, and lists what is due in each.
    • Gaps and oddities. The vault looks over what you have recorded and points at what stands out: belongings above a contents limit you noted, no home or contents policy recorded, a life policy with no beneficiaries, a policy renewing soon with no claims line, papers that all expire within a month, no recent backup. Prompts to look, not verdicts.
    • Crisis drills. Pick a bad day (a fire tonight, a lost wallet or phone, a hospital stay, away for two weeks, you cannot be reached) and see a readiness ring and a checklist of what your records would give you, with a link to fix each gap.
    • Recovery kit. The one thing nobody can fix for you is a lost passphrase. Split yours into 2 to 10 printed shares (Shamir secret sharing, with random coefficients from your browser) so that any chosen few, say 3 of 5, rebuild it and one alone reveals nothing. Every share has a checksum, a set id and a printable card. Practise a rebuild against your vault without showing the passphrase, and rebuild from the lock screen if you ever need to. The app stores only the date you made them.

    How it protects you

    • AES-256-GCM encryption through your browser's built-in WebCrypto. Every new 1.1 record (policies, medicines, digital accounts, the emergency plan, reminders, extra photos and receipts) lives inside the same encrypted vault.
    • The key is derived from your passphrase with PBKDF2-SHA256, 600,000 iterations and a random salt per vault. Imported files may use between 100,000 and 2,000,000 iterations; anything outside that is refused.
    • Scans, photos and receipts inside imported files must be real pictures (PNG, JPEG, WebP, GIF, BMP) or PDFs.
    • GCM authenticates everything: a damaged or edited file is refused rather than half-opened, and the file header is bound to the data. Imported files are strictly checked before use.
    • Every save is read back and checked, and the last good copy is kept.
    • A passphrase strength meter on creation, with a minimum you cannot skip.
    • Auto-lock after inactivity (1 to 30 minutes), and a lock button. Locking removes your data from memory and the screen.
    • Your passphrase is never stored. Only the encrypted file is saved, in your browser's own storage.
    • A strict content security policy blocks every network connection. The app contains no code that can send data anywhere.

    • Your vault lives in this browser's storage on this computer. Clearing site data or reinstalling would erase it, so save a backup file now and then, or set a mirror file. The app reminds you.
