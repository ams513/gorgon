    About Math

    Math is a maths and science tutor that works without an internet connection. Its teacher, Madeline, walks you through a worked example, then lets you try. If you get stuck she gives a nudge, then a hint, then the next step. If your answer is wrong, she looks at what you actually typed and tells you what probably went wrong: added the denominators, dropped a minus sign, worked left to right instead of following the order of operations, forgot to square the speed, mixed up kilometres and metres.

    New in version 1.10: four numbers on the first screen, a weekly target, CSV in and out, repeating homework and reviews about to fade

    • Four numbers on the home card: how many items are late, how many are due in the next 7 days, how many skill reviews come due in the next 7 days, and how many problems are still owed to reach this week's target (with the number a day). Each one opens the list behind it. Under them, one plain line compares this week with last week: problems done, and the right-first-time percentage. These are your own recorded numbers; nothing is estimated and no money or time saving is claimed.
    • Weekly target: pick 20 to 150 problems a week (the default is five days of your daily goal). The This week page shows a bar and what is owed per remaining day, including today.
    • Reviews about to fade: a page listing the skills you have practised whose review comes due within 7 days, soonest first, each with a Practise button, a Review now button and a printable list.
    • Repeating homework: mark an item "repeats every week". When you tick it off, next week's copy is added once.
    • Records and backup page: save your homework and tests, practice log, mistakes, and skills with next reviews as CSV files that open in any spreadsheet; bring a homework list in from a CSV file (rows with a missing title or a bad date are skipped and counted); print a one-page progress sheet; back up and restore. A backup file saved by Math 1.7 or later opens in this one (checked with real files from 1.7 and 1.9). Spreadsheet formulas in text are defused when saved.
    • The menu row on phones now fades at the edge to show it scrolls sideways.
    • All of this is saved with the learner's other data, works offline and is part of the full version. Not translated yet: the new screens show in English in every screen language.

    Answers checked exactly

    • Every problem is generated fresh and checked by the app's own exact maths engine. Equivalent answers count: 1/2, 0.5 and 2/4 are all accepted where the question allows it, and Madeline asks you to simplify when the skill is about simplifying.

    • Physical constants are the CODATA 2018 values; atomic masses are IUPAC standard values.

    • Contest result files are signed so casual hand edits are detected, and organisers re-score every answer. The signature is an integrity check, not proof of who played: its key comes from the event pack every player holds, so a technically skilled player could still make a file. For high-stakes events, supervise the room as you would for a paper test.
    • Playing on itch: progress is saved for this page in your browser. Use backups or the offline download to keep it long-term.
    • Simulators use textbook physics: a point-sized ball, flat ground for the target, simple air drag that grows with speed squared, and an ideal pendulum (no friction). Installing needs the web version in a browser that supports installing web apps; inside the itch.io page the browser may not offer it.
    • The skills are deep rather than exhaustive (for example, no proofs-by-induction practice yet). The calculus lab handles polynomials only, and sealed backups need a browser with WebCrypto (all current ones).
